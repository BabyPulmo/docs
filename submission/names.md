# International product names — pediatric cough-AI

> **Final pick (selected 2026-05-24): Baby Pulmo.** Domain `babypulmo.com` purchased. The 16 candidates below are kept for posterity / future sub-branding decisions.

16 candidates considered for a globally-deployable rebrand. Bangla in-country, neutral name for India / Pakistan / Nigeria / Indonesia / Philippines and for clinician + investor audiences.

## Naming bar

1. Pronounceable on first try in English, Hindi/Urdu/Bangla, Indonesian, Yoruba/Hausa, Tagalog.
2. ≤3 syllables. Memorable after one meeting.
3. No collision with **ResApp** (Pfizer), **Hyfe**, **Cohort.ai**, **Eko Health** — the four direct cough/respiratory-AI incumbents.
4. Hints at *what we do* (cough / breath / listen / care / child) without being literal.
5. Domain hints flagged; **user must verify** at a registrar before committing.
6. Trademark hints flagged; **user must run USPTO + Madrid Protocol search** before committing.

## Voice / Listening family

The "Kantho" lineage — what makes this product distinctive is that *it listens to the cough*. These names honor that.

### 1. Auris
- **Meaning:** Latin for "ear" — used in medical anatomy (auris externa, auris media).
- **Pronunciation:** AW-riss. Two syllables. Universally clean.
- **Pros:** Clinical credibility, hints at listening, short, brandable. Tells the "we hear what doctors can't be there to hear" story.
- **Cons / vet:** Auris Health was a robotic-surgery company acquired by J&J for $3.4B in 2019 — same Latin root, different category but **trademark proximity is a real risk in healthcare class 10/44**. Domain `auris.ai` very likely taken; `auris.health` worth checking. *Vet hard before falling in love.*

### 2. Hark
- **Meaning:** English "to listen attentively" (archaic/poetic).
- **Pronunciation:** HARK. One syllable. Very memorable.
- **Pros:** Punchy, English-speakers know it, has a moral-imperative tone ("hark, the child"). Easy to trademark search.
- **Cons / vet:** Quite Anglo — may feel less natural in non-English-speaking markets. Several small SaaS companies use Hark in unrelated categories. **Check `hark.ai` / `hark.health`**.

### 3. Sonus
- **Meaning:** Latin "sound."
- **Pronunciation:** SO-nuss. Two syllables.
- **Pros:** Clean, clinical, audio-tech feel.
- **Cons / vet:** **Sonus Networks** was a major VoIP company (now Ribbon Communications) — name was widely used in tech. Sonus Hearing Aids and Sonus Pharmaceuticals also exist. **Trademark space is crowded; deprioritize unless you love it.**

### 4. Audera
- **Meaning:** Invented — `audere` (Latin: to dare) + audio root.
- **Pronunciation:** ow-DEH-ra. Three syllables. Warm, slightly Spanish/Italian feel.
- **Pros:** Brandable, friendly, made-up so trademark headroom is wider, hints at audio + bravery (good for a "we go where doctors can't" story).
- **Cons / vet:** Audera is an existing audiology equipment brand (Grason-Stadler) — direct conflict in the hearing/audiometric space. **Avoid unless you can clear that.**

### 5. Listen.health
- **Meaning:** Domain-as-name. Literal.
- **Pronunciation:** LIS-en-dot-health.
- **Pros:** Self-explanatory, modern .health TLD, marketing writes itself.
- **Cons / vet:** Gimmicky if .health goes out of fashion; harder to say out loud than to write. Confirm the literal `listen.health` domain is available.

## Breath / Lung family

Telegraphs the respiratory focus — investors and clinicians immediately know the category.

### 6. Respira
- **Meaning:** Spanish / Italian / Portuguese for "breathe" (imperative).
- **Pronunciation:** reh-SPEE-rah. Three syllables. Beautiful in Spanish/Italian/Portuguese; easy in English; easy in South Asian languages.
- **Pros:** Warm, parent-facing, multilingual native feel, telegraphs respiratory perfectly. Great for the LATAM expansion that *will* happen if this scales.
- **Cons / vet:** Respira Therapeutics existed (inhaled-drug delivery, acquired 2016) — same therapy area, **trademark vet is mandatory**. Domain `respira.ai` and `respira.health` likely contested.

### 7. Pulmo.ai
- **Meaning:** Latin root "pulmo" (lung) + .ai TLD.
- **Pronunciation:** POOL-mo-dot-AI. Two syllables.
- **Pros:** Direct, clinical, obvious category fit, .ai TLD signals AI-native.
- **Cons / vet:** "Pulmo" is used by many products (Pulmozyme, Pulmocort, PulmoLife). The .ai TLD differentiates but doesn't trademark-protect. **Run a class-10 + class-44 search.**

### 8. Inspira
- **Meaning:** Latin "breathe in"; also "inspire" double meaning.
- **Pronunciation:** in-SPEE-rah. Three syllables. Travels everywhere.
- **Pros:** Aspirational + medical double meaning, warm, multilingual.
- **Cons / vet:** Inspira Health Network is a large US hospital system. Inspira Bioscience is a small therapeutics co. **Very crowded namespace in US healthcare — deprioritize.**

### 9. Aera
- **Meaning:** Latin "air."
- **Pronunciation:** AIR-ah. Two syllables. Universally clean.
- **Pros:** Soft, brandable, short, hints at breath without being on-the-nose.
- **Cons / vet:** **Aera Technologies** is a known enterprise-AI/decision-intelligence company — same word, different category. Crowded for trademark; vet class 9/10/44 carefully.

### 10. Lumen
- **Meaning:** Latin "light"; in medicine, "internal diameter of an airway" (clinical pun).
- **Pronunciation:** LOO-men. Two syllables.
- **Pros:** Beautiful double meaning (light + airway), short, clinical.
- **Cons / vet:** **Lumen** is the metabolic-tracker breath device (~$200M valuation) — direct conflict in *breath-analysis hardware*. **Hard pass** — would actively confuse the market.

## Child / Pediatric family

Telegraphs the under-5 focus where 740k/year die — the most emotionally resonant angle for press and donors.

### 11. Nido
- **Meaning:** Spanish/Italian "nest."
- **Pronunciation:** NEE-doh. Two syllables. Universal.
- **Pros:** Warm, parent-facing, exactly the right emotional register for a tool a mother uses.
- **Cons / vet:** **Nestlé NIDO** is a global infant-formula brand — direct conflict in the *pediatric / infant* space. **Hard pass** unless you want a cease-and-desist on day one.

### 12. Pedia.ai
- **Meaning:** "Pediatric" shortened + .ai TLD.
- **Pronunciation:** PEE-dee-ah-dot-AI.
- **Pros:** Direct, descriptive, clinical, obvious B2G/B2H pitch.
- **Cons / vet:** "Pedia" is heavily used (PediaSure, Pediatrix, PediaCare). The .ai differentiates but the namespace is shopworn. Feels generic for a $100M-aspiration brand.

### 13. Karuna
- **Meaning:** Sanskrit/Pali "compassion" — a core Buddhist/Hindu virtue.
- **Pronunciation:** kah-ROO-nah. Three syllables. Easy in South Asia, Southeast Asia, and Africa; slightly exotic-but-positive in the West.
- **Pros:** Beautiful meaning, travels naturally across South Asia + Southeast Asia (your Phase 2–4 markets), emotionally resonant for a child-saving product. NRB-friendly: a Bangladeshi-led brand with a Sanskrit name lands well in India and global investor decks.
- **Cons / vet:** **Karuna Therapeutics** is a NASDAQ-listed schizophrenia-drug company (~$3B) — same word, *adjacent* healthcare but different therapy area. Trademark conflict in class 5 (pharmaceuticals) is real but Baby Pulmo is in class 10/44 (medical devices/services) — **trademark lawyer call needed but not necessarily blocked**. Some Westerners may mispronounce as "ka-ROO-na."

### 14. Vela
- **Meaning:** Spanish "candle / vigil / to watch over"; Latin "sail."
- **Pronunciation:** VAY-lah. Two syllables. Universal.
- **Pros:** Pediatric-watchful connotation ("watching over your child at night"), short, soft, brandable. Vela is also a southern constellation — quiet sky-watch metaphor for an always-on monitor.
- **Cons / vet:** Several small companies use Vela (Vela Diagnostics — molecular-dx, Vela Software — Constellation Software brand). **Vela Diagnostics is the one to worry about** — same broad healthcare space. Vet class 10/44.

### 15. Vyana
- **Meaning:** Sanskrit "the breath that pervades the body" — one of the five pranas in Ayurveda. Combines the breath/lung *and* the South-Asian-resonance angles.
- **Pronunciation:** VYAH-nah or vee-AH-nah. Two-to-three syllables. Travels well in South Asia; novel but pronounceable in the West.
- **Pros:** Genuinely fresh — almost zero healthtech competition for this word. Tells both the "we measure breath" *and* "we come from this part of the world" story without locking into a single language.
- **Cons / vet:** Unfamiliar to non-South-Asians on first hearing (mitigated by the strong meaning, which is a fun first-meeting anecdote). Trademark headroom likely wide. Domain `vyana.ai` / `vyana.health` worth checking immediately.

## Hybrid (Child + Lung) family

A single candidate that fuses the pediatric and respiratory angles into one phrase.

### 16. Baby Pulmo
- **Meaning:** English "baby" + Latin "pulmo" (lung). Self-explanatory: pediatric lung product.
- **Pronunciation:** BAY-bee PULL-mo. Three syllables. Universally easy.
- **Pros:** Crystal-clear category in two words — no tagline required. Warm ("baby") + clinical ("pulmo") at once, the same emotional pairing as Eko or PillPack. Almost impossible to misspell. Travels: "baby" is universally understood; "pulmo" is a recognizable medical root globally.
- **Cons / vet:** **Descriptiveness risk at USPTO** — examiners can refuse "merely descriptive" marks under Lanham Act §2(e)(1). The save is that "pulmo" is Latin/suggestive rather than the English "lung," which most likely puts it on the Principal Register as a *suggestive* mark, but expect a possible office action. "Baby + medical" is a crowded namespace (BabyCenter, BabyBjorn, Baby Brezza) — no direct cough-AI conflicts, but examiners look at likelihood of confusion across the whole baby category. "Pulmo" appears in many class-5 pharma marks (Pulmozyme, Pulmocort, PulmoLife) — different class from ours (9/10/44) but examiners do cross-class analysis. Two-word names mean compound domains (`babypulmo.ai`, `babypulmo.com`) rather than a single-word URL.
- **IP take:** See `ip-strategy.md` — full strategy assumes Baby Pulmo as the lead candidate with Vyana and Respira as backups; the same $1.5–2.5k attorney clearance opinion covers all three.

## Top 4 — my recommendation

After weighing pronunciation across target markets, distance from incumbents (ResApp / Hyfe / Cohort.ai / Eko / Lumen / Nestlé NIDO), narrative fit, and trademark headroom, the four I'd actually take to a registrar:

### 🥇 Baby Pulmo
Best self-explanatory. The only candidate where category, audience, and brand all collapse into two words — a doctor, a parent, a UNICEF officer, a Series A investor all *immediately know what this is*. Warm + clinical pairing matches the most successful healthtech naming pattern (Eko, Forward, Olive). Main risk is a USPTO §2(e)(1) descriptiveness office action; the Latin/suggestive nature of "pulmo" most likely survives that, but attorney clearance is mandatory. Tagline: **"Baby Pulmo — when a child's cough needs more than guesswork."**

### 🥈 Vyana
Best trademark headroom. Sanskrit "the pervading breath" perfectly captures *what the product measures*; it's culturally rooted in your home region without being locked into Bangla; the trademark/domain namespace looks genuinely open (rare in healthtech); and "the breath that pervades the body" is a *memorable first-meeting story*. Strongest fallback if Baby Pulmo fails clearance. Tagline: **"Vyana — the breath, heard."**

### 🥉 Respira
Best parent-facing. Native multilingual feel across LATAM, South Asia, Southeast Asia, and Africa. Warmest of the candidates. The downside is a real trademark vet needed (Respira Therapeutics), but the word is generic enough that surviving that vet is plausible. Tagline: **"Respira — when a child's breath needs a second listener."**

### 🏅 Karuna
Best mission-facing. The "compassion" meaning is a gift to fundraising and B2G pitches — UNICEF, WHO, Save the Children decks practically write themselves. Risk is Karuna Therapeutics (pharma) trademark; opportunity is that Baby Pulmo is in a different IP class. Tagline: **"Karuna — listening, where doctors can't be."**

### Honorable mentions
- **Auris** — clinical perfection but Auris Health (J&J) makes the trademark fight hard.
- **Vela** — beautiful, pediatric-watchful, but Vela Diagnostics overlaps.
- **Hark** — punchy but Anglo-centric for a global LMIC product.

## What you should do next

1. **Pick 1–2 favorites** from the Top 3 (or honorable mentions).
2. **Run a domain check** at Namecheap / Cloudflare / Porkbun: `<name>.ai`, `<name>.health`, `<name>.com`, `<name>.io`.
3. **Run a USPTO + Madrid Protocol trademark search** in classes 9 (software), 10 (medical devices), 44 (medical services).
4. **Sanity-check the name with two native speakers** of Hindi/Urdu, Indonesian, and Yoruba — make sure it doesn't unintentionally mean something embarrassing.
5. When you've picked one, ask me to: (a) rewrite the README/summary with the new brand, (b) propose a GitHub org migration plan, (c) draft a "Baby Pulmo → \<new name\> (Bangladesh edition)" bilingual lockup.

## What I deliberately did NOT do

- I did **not** verify domain availability or trademark status — those require live network calls and a lawyer.
- I did **not** propose logo / visual identity / color palette.
- I did **not** rename the codebase or the GitHub org. The org name `BabyPulmo` is still the right one for the in-country brand; the international name layers on top, not replaces.
